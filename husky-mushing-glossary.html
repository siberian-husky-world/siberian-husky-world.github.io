Musher's Glossary

Learn 20 mushing terms with interactive flip cards. Gee, haw, gangline, booties — the words you hear on the trail, explained. Use the arrow keys to navigate, Space or Enter to flip a card.

Card 1 of 20
01 Gee tap or press Space to flip
turn right

The command given to the lead dogs to turn right. Paired with 'haw' for left turns.

02 Haw tap or press Space to flip
turn left

The command for lead dogs to turn left. Every musher learns gee and haw first.

03 Hike tap or press Space to flip
go / start running

The command to start moving. Also 'line out' prepares the team to move.

04 Whoa tap or press Space to flip
stop

The command to stop the team. A critical safety command on any trail.

05 Lead dog tap or press Space to flip
front pair

The two dogs at the front who set the pace and follow the musher's commands. The smartest and most reliable dogs in the team.

06 Wheel dog tap or press Space to flip
rear pair

The two dogs closest to the sled. They provide pulling power and help steer the sled around tight corners.

07 Swing dog tap or press Space to flip
middle pair

Dogs positioned between the wheel and lead dogs. They provide power and help the lead dogs swing the team around turns.

08 Gangline tap or press Space to flip
main tow line

The central rope or cable that connects all dogs to the sled. Each dog's tug line clips into the gangline.

09 Neckline tap or press Space to flip
side connector

A short line connecting each dog's collar to the gangline, keeping the team aligned and preventing tangles.

10 Tug line tap or press Space to flip
individual tow

The short line from each dog's harness to the gangline. The dog pulls against this line.

11 Sled tap or press Space to flip
the vehicle

The mushing vehicle — a wooden or synthetic runners-based platform the musher stands on the back of.

12 Scooter / rig tap or press Space to flip
dry-land gear

A wheeled scooter or three-wheeled rig used for mushing on dry ground. Common for canicross and urban mushing.

13 Canicross tap or press Space to flip
run with dog

A dry-land mushing sport where the dog pulls a person wearing a waist belt connected by a bungee line. No sled needed.

14 Bikejoring tap or press Space to flip
bike with dog

A dog (or two) pulls a bicycle via a bungee line. The dog runs ahead and the rider pedals to assist on hills.

15 Dryland tap or press Space to flip
no snow needed

Mushing without snow — using scooter, bike or canicross gear on dirt, gravel or grassy trails.

16 Booties tap or press Space to flip
dog footwear

Protective fabric boots for dogs' paws. Used on icy trails to prevent snowballs and cuts from sharp ice.

17 Drop line tap or press Space to flip
restraint line

A short line attached to the dog's harness during rest stops. Lets a handler work with one dog while the team rests.

18 Snub line tap or press Space to flip
parking brake

A line attached to a tree or stake to hold the team stationary, essential during rest breaks and race starts.

19 Pedaling tap or press Space to flip
one-foot push

Pushing with one foot while standing on the other runner. Used to help the team on flat ground or soft snow.

20 Runners tap or press Space to flip
sled blades

The two smooth strips at the bottom of the sled that glide over snow. Usually made of plastic or metal.

How to use: Flip each card to see the definition. Navigate with the buttons or keyboard (Left/Right arrows, Space to flip). Terms are from recreational mushing and dryland canicross. No data is stored or sent. Read about Husky escape behaviour
