Ruffwear Ridgeline Harness Review 2026: Does the Magnetic-Buckle Harness Hold Up to an Escape-Artist Husky?

This Ruffwear Ridgeline harness review answers one question for Husky owners: will the magnetic buckles and the cut keep a dog who likes to reverse out of things? The buckles are the less worrying part. Whether the harness stays on depends on how its straps sit on your particular dog, and I found no independent escape test that settles it. I have not tested the harness myself; this review is built from the maker's specifications, published reviews and a fit routine you can run at home.

Short answer: consider the Ridgeline if you want a rugged, weather-resistant trail harness with buckles you can work in gloves. Do not buy it as a proven escape-proof harness. Measure first, test the fit indoors, and keep a backup connection on a Husky who has slipped gear before.

Do the Fidlock magnetic buckles hold under pulling?

The word “magnetic” sounds as if a strong pull could separate the two halves. The design works differently. The magnet guides the parts into place and a mechanical catch carries the load. Fidlock's own V-buckle demonstration describes the family as self-securing: the more load on the fastener, the harder it is to open. Ruffwear makes the same point about the Ridgeline and says it tested the buckles in its lab and in the field. Both are manufacturer statements, not third-party failure tests.

Opening is where your attention belongs. Ruffwear says the buckles can be released with one hand, even in gloves. Learn exactly what motion releases yours before the first walk. My own inference is that any easy-release hardware is worth checking after a dog has crashed through brush or wrestled with a friend, so run a hand over all four buckles before you set off again.

Magnets also invite grit and ice questions. I found no independent test of these buckles packed with snow or sand. After a dirty outing, rinse them, close each one and tug it before the next walk.

Does the Ridgeline stop an escape-artist Husky?

A Husky rarely escapes by breaking a buckle. The usual harness trick is to stop, lower the head and reverse until the gear slides forward over the shoulders. Strap position and adjustment decide that contest. Treeline Review's dog harness guide shows the Ridgeline from above with a long back panel and two straps around the torso, and separately notes that it shares sizing concerns with other Ruffwear harnesses.

Ruffwear's Ridgeline description talks about materials, buckles, handle and leash points. It makes no escape-proof claim. I did not find a Husky-specific escape report either way. That leaves the headline question open: the hardware gives no obvious reason for concern, but the fit on your dog is unproven until you have checked it.

A five-minute back-out test before the first walk

Do this indoors or inside a securely closed area. Never run it near a road or an open gate.

  1. Fit the harness and let your dog move for a few minutes so the coat and straps settle.
  2. Close each buckle again and tug the two halves apart without touching the pull tab.
  3. With a leash on the back ring, call your dog gently away from you so you can watch the harness under light, steady tension. Do not yank.
  4. Watch the torso straps. If they travel toward the elbows or the chest panel climbs the neck, adjust and repeat.
  5. Stop if your dog shows discomfort or panic, and try again on another day with treats.

For a Husky with a past escape, add a second connection: a short safety strap or carabiner from the harness to a well-fitted flat collar. If one piece slips, the other is still attached to your leash.

Pulling, hiking and everyday use

A trail harness with a front attachment can help with steering, but gear does not teach a Husky to walk on a loose leash. We cover that split in no-pull harnesses for Huskies. If your plan is canicross, bikejoring or skijoring, compare a purpose-built pulling design such as the one in our Freemotion Harness Pro review. The Ridgeline is presented as a hiking and expedition harness.

The coat matters for comfort. Part the fur under the straps after longer outings and look for matting or flattened, tangled patches. Owners of longer-coated dogs may also want our wooly Husky grooming schedule.

Fit changes and care

One Husky can need new settings as the coat thickens or sheds. Recheck the adjusters whenever the coat changes noticeably and after washing. Ruffwear's care steps are to secure the fasteners, wash in cold water on a gentle cycle with mild detergent and air dry.

Fit surprises happen across dog shapes, and the sizing concerns Treeline mentions are a good reason to confirm the return terms before the tags come off. Try the harness indoors first, while it is still clean enough to send back.

Verdict: who should buy the Ridgeline?

Reasonable choice: you hike in wet or cold conditions, you value buckles that work with gloves, your Husky fits the chart cleanly, and you are willing to test and adjust.

Look elsewhere first: your dog backs out often and you want a design its maker presents as escape-resistant, you need a dedicated pulling harness, or $179.99 is more than you want to risk on an unproven fit.

So does it hold up to an escape-artist Husky? The buckle design is promising on paper. The harness as a whole has not been shown to defeat a determined backer-out, so keep the second connection until your own repeated checks give you reason to trust it, and even then it costs little to leave it on.

Ruffwear Ridgeline harness FAQ

Is the Ruffwear Ridgeline harness escape-proof?

I found no independent escape test of the Ridgeline, and the product page does not call it escape-proof. Treat it as a secure-fit candidate that still needs a fit check, a calm back-out test and a backup connection to the collar.

Can a Husky pull the magnetic buckles open?

Ruffwear says the Fidlock lock holds more firmly under force. The magnets guide the two halves together and a mechanical catch holds them. The release uses a pull tab, so glance at the tabs after thick brush or rough play.

What size Ridgeline fits a Siberian Husky?

Size by the girth around the widest part of the rib cage, not by breed or body weight. The chart runs from XXS at 13 in to L/XL at 42 in. Between sizes, Ruffwear says to choose the smaller one.

How much does the Ridgeline harness cost?

The list price on Ruffwear's US store is $179.99. Sales and stock change, so check the current listing before you plan around a number.

Can you wash the Ridgeline harness?

Ruffwear's care steps are: secure the fasteners, wash in cold water on a gentle cycle with mild detergent, then air dry.

Should I clip the leash to the front or the back?

The harness has three leash attachment points. Pick by the activity and by how your dog walks, and practise loose-leash walking either way.
