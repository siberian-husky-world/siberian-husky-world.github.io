Freemotion Harness Pro Review 2026: Is the New Pro Pulling Harness Worth It for Huskies in Canicross, Bikejoring and Skijoring?

What Non-stop dogwear changed in its top pulling harness, how it compares with four other models from the same maker, and which Huskies will actually notice the difference.

Home›Blog›Freemotion Harness Pro Review

The Non-stop dogwear Freemotion Harness Pro is the Norwegian brand's new top pulling harness, launched in September 2026 for dogs that run in front of a runner, a bike or a skier. Husky owners keep asking the same thing: is a $149.99 harness really better for a Siberian than the Freemotion 5.0 that has been the default canicross harness for years? The honest answer depends on your dog's neck, how hard and how often it pulls, and which sport you do.

Quick answer: The Freemotion Harness Pro is worth it for a Husky that pulls steadily in harness several times a week, especially in bikejoring or skijoring, and for owners who want a lighter, closer fit with slimmer neck options. For an occasional canicross dog, a beginner, or a Husky that is still learning to pull in a straight line, the Freemotion 5.0 or the shorter Rush harness does the same basic job for less. For a dog pulling a sled in a team, an X-back such as the Nansen nome harness 5.0 is the better tool.

What is the Freemotion Harness Pro?

The Freemotion Harness Pro is a pulling harness for sports where the line runs higher than the dog's back: canicross, bikejoring, scooterjoring and skijoring. It keeps the Y-shaped front of the original Freemotion and adds a new seam construction, slimmer neck options and reinforced wear zones.

According to Non-stop dogwear's product page, the new CleanSeam construction stitches nearly all seams from the inside out, so the side touching the dog is smoother. The page lists four adjustment points and one attachment point, Hypalon reinforcements on exposed areas, 3M reflective details and a single colour, black. The trade magazine Pet Business World reported the launch in September 2026 and quoted chief executive Tonje Wahl Evensen, who said the harness combines what the company learned at the highest level of dog-powered sports.

Freemotion Harness Pro vs Freemotion 5.0: what actually changed?

The Pro and the 5.0 share the same Freemotion fit principle and the same adjustment points. The Pro swaps the 5.0's thicker padding for thinner, lighter padding with CleanSeam construction, adds S neck sizes and Hypalon reinforcements, and costs $50 more.

The Freemotion 5.0 is the latest version of a harness Non-stop dogwear started prototyping in 2006, and it remains on sale next to the Pro. The brand's own FAQ on the Pro page sums up the differences, and one of them is easy to miss: on the 5.0 both the side straps and the back straps are replaceable, while on the Pro only the back straps are.

1. Freemotion Harness Pro: best for serious pulling Huskies

The Pro makes the most sense for a Husky that already pulls hard and straight, several times a week, ahead of a bike or skis. That is where a lighter, closer harness with anti-slip straps that stay put under load earns its price. Early buyers rate it highly, with an average of 4.5 out of 5 from 38 ratings on the product page, though that is a small sample for a harness only on sale since September 2026.

  • Lighter, closer fit with CleanSeam construction against the coat
  • S neck options in sizes 5 to 7 for slim-necked dogs
  • Hypalon reinforcement where the harness wears
  • Anti-slip side and back straps for a stable fit under pull
  • $50 more than the Freemotion 5.0
  • Side straps are not replaceable, only the back strap
  • Black only, so less visible than the bright 5.0 colours apart from its reflective details

2. Freemotion Harness 5.0: best value for most Husky owners

If your Husky does canicross at weekends and the odd bikejoring session, the 5.0 is the sensible choice. It uses the same fit principle as the Pro, the same sizes and the same adjustment points, and every strap that wears out can be replaced. The thicker padding is not a drawback for a dog that pulls a few times a week.

  • $50 cheaper than the Pro with the same Freemotion fit
  • Side and back straps replaceable
  • Several bright colours
  • No slim-neck sizes
  • Bulkier padding than the Pro

3. Rush harness: best first harness for a young Husky

The Rush is a short harness that slips over the head and closes with side buckles. Non-stop dogwear designs it for canicross, bikejoring, skijoring and hiking, and also for introducing a dog to pulling sports. For a young Husky still learning to run in front, it is a cheaper way to find out whether your dog loves pulling before you spend $149.99.

  • Cheapest Freemotion-style option at $74.99
  • Fast on and off, fits short-backed dogs
  • Sizes 1 to 8 in teal or black/orange
  • Shorter body coverage than a full Freemotion
  • Less suited to a dog that pulls very hard and steadily

4. Nansen nome harness 5.0: best for Huskies in a sled team

The Freemotion family is built for a high pulling point. When your Husky pulls a sled or a pulka with the load low behind it, an X-back harness spreads the force better. The Nansen nome harness 5.0 is Non-stop dogwear's X-back, and the brand says it is the harness Thomas Wærner used when he won the Iditarod in 2020.

  • Built for sled pulling at a low pulling point
  • Proven in long-distance sled dog racing
  • Lowest price of the five at $69.99
  • Not designed for canicross or bikejoring
  • Black only

5. Freemotion harness WD: best for strength training

The WD is the working-dog version of the Freemotion. Two side rings take the separately sold Pulling attachment WD, so the same harness can also pull a tire or chain with a low pulling point. For a Husky that does dryland strength work between snow seasons, it covers both jobs.

  • Heavy-duty materials for resistance training
  • One harness for high and low pulling with the add-on
  • Low-pull attachment costs extra
  • Olive only, sizes 4 to 8

How does the Freemotion Harness Pro fit a Siberian Husky?

Fit decides more than the model. Non-stop dogwear's fitting guide asks for two fingers, stacked on top of each other, between the top of the neck and the harness on a medium-sized dog, with the dog standing and the harness held in a pulling position. A pulling harness should sit quite snug around the neck.

Measure under the ruff, not on top of it

A Husky's thick neck ruff can make a neck look much wider than it is. Press the fur flat when you measure, then use the size guide. The S sizes have a smaller neck opening but the same length as the regular size, so a 6 and a 6S fit the same body length. The brand says the slim versions were added for dogs with narrow necks, such as hounds and German pointers. Many Huskies will measure into the regular size, so measure your dog instead of assuming.

Watch the coat under the straps

Hours in any harness can press the undercoat flat along the straps. The CleanSeam surface is smoother, but it does not change that. If your Husky has a long coat, comb the harness line after every run, as we describe in our guide to wooly Husky grooming. During a coat blow, loose undercoat packs under the straps faster. Canicross, bikejoring or skijoring: does the sport change the answer?

Yes. The faster and longer the dog pulls at full effort, the more a lighter, more stable harness matters. That makes bikejoring and skijoring the strongest case for the Pro, and casual canicross the weakest.

  • Canicross: the runner wears a waist belt, the dog wears a pulling harness, and a bungee line connects them, as the Wikipedia entry on canicross explains. It also notes that the first canicross dogs were sledding and spitz types such as the Husky. At running pace, the 5.0 and the Pro do the same job for most dogs.
  • Bikejoring and scooterjoring: speeds are higher and the dog pulls for longer stretches. A Husky that keeps the line tight benefits most from the Pro's lower weight and stable straps.
  • Skijoring: long, steady pulls on snow, and one of the three sports the brand designed the Pro for. A dog that pulls the whole way gets the most from the closer fit.
  • Sled or pulka: low pulling point, so choose an X-back or a dedicated pulka harness, not the Pro.

How much pulling work your dog can handle should follow its overall fitness. Our article on how much exercise a Siberian Husky needs helps you build the base before you add speed.

When is the Freemotion Harness Pro not worth it?

Skip the Pro if your Husky is not yet pulling in harness, if you mainly walk, or if a cheaper Freemotion already fits well. In each case you pay for performance the dog will not use.

  • For everyday walks: a pulling harness tells a Husky that pulling is the job. If leash manners are the problem, read our guide to no-pull harnesses for Huskies and keep the sport harness for sport.
  • For a beginner dog: start with the Rush or the 5.0 until you know your Husky enjoys pulling.
  • If you need colour: the Pro only comes in black. The brightly coloured 5.0 is easier to spot on a trail.
  • If straps wear out fast: on the 5.0 you can replace the side straps too.

Care, durability and what you get in the box

The care routine is simple. Close the hook-and-loop fasteners, put the harness in a laundry bag and wash it gently at 30 °C, or wash it by hand, and let it air dry. The replacement back strap also fits the Freemotion 5.0 in sizes 6 to 9, and it may need to be cut to length and the end sealed with a lighter. On the US store the Pro comes with a two-year warranty and 60-day free returns, which leaves time to check the fit on a few real runs.

Freemotion Harness Pro FAQ

Is the Freemotion Harness Pro good for Huskies?

Yes, for Huskies that pull steadily in canicross, bikejoring or skijoring. It is a high-pulling-point harness with a Y-shaped front that leaves the shoulders free. For sled teams an X-back harness is the better choice, and for beginners the cheaper Freemotion 5.0 or Rush harness is enough.

What is the difference between the Freemotion Harness Pro and the 5.0?

The Pro has thinner, lighter padding with CleanSeam construction, slim S neck sizes from 5 to 7, Hypalon reinforcements and anti-slip straps. The 5.0 has thicker padding, more colours, replaceable side and back straps, and costs $99.99 instead of $149.99.

What size Freemotion Harness Pro does a Siberian Husky need?

There is no single Husky size. The Pro uses the same sizing system as the Freemotion 5.0, so start with the brand's size guide, then measure the neck with the ruff pressed flat to choose between a regular and an S size in sizes 5 to 7.

Can I use the Freemotion Harness Pro for walking?

The brand lists hiking as a secondary use, but a pulling harness encourages a Husky to pull. For daily walks and leash training, use a walking harness and keep the Pro for sport.

Can a Husky pull a sled in the Freemotion Harness Pro?

It is designed for activities where the pulling point is higher than the dog's back. For a sled or pulka with a low pulling point, an X-back harness such as the Nansen nome harness 5.0 is the better tool.

How do I wash the Freemotion Harness Pro?

Close the hook-and-loop fasteners, use a laundry bag and a gentle machine wash at 30 °C, or wash by hand. Do not tumble dry.

Verdict: who should buy the Freemotion Harness Pro?

The Freemotion Harness Pro is a refined version of a proven design, not a different kind of harness. For a Husky that pulls hard and often in bikejoring or skijoring, the lighter build, slimmer neck options and stable straps are worth the extra $50. For a weekend canicross dog, the Freemotion 5.0 gives you most of the same fit for less, and for a sled team the Nansen nome is still the right tool. Measure your dog under the ruff, try the matcher above, and buy for the sport your Husky actually does.
